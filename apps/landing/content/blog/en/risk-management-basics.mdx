Most trading education focuses on entries and exits. Prop firm education should focus on **not breaking the rules** while entries and exits take care of themselves.

Here are the four numbers you actually need to track.

## 1. Drawdown velocity

Drawdown velocity is how fast your account is approaching the max drawdown limit. A slow drift is manageable. A fast spike on a single bad day is what ends challenges.

```python
drawdown_velocity = (equity_high - current_equity) / days_elapsed
safe_pace = total_drawdown_limit / total_days_allowed
```

If your velocity exceeds the safe pace, you're on a path to breach — even if you're still profitable.

## 2. Daily loss proximity

How close are you to the max daily loss right now? This is the number that should be on your screen at all times during a session.

<Callout type="warning">
  Most platforms show your P&L. Very few show your proximity to the daily loss limit. Those are not the same thing, and confusing them is the #1 reason traders get surprised by a blown account.
</Callout>

## 3. Consistency score

Many firms penalize "one big day" traders. If 60% of your profit came from a single session, some firms won't fund you — even if you hit the target.

Track the distribution of your daily P&L as a percentage of total profit. No single day should represent more than 30–40% of your total.

## 4. Risk-of-ruin

Given your current win rate, average win, and average loss, what is the statistical probability of hitting max drawdown before hitting the profit target?

This number should scare you into trading smaller. It usually does.
